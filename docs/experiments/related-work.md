| Wiesner et al., "Let's Wait Awhile: How Temporal Workload Shifting Can Reduce Carbon Emissions in the Cloud", Middleware 2021, https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.13234 | Analyses the potential of shifting flexible workloads in time in Germany, Great Britain, France and California (2020); runs scenarios to see how time constraints, scheduling strategy and forecast accuracy matter. Abstract gives no savings number. | Abstract opened | We use 2023-2026 data, a strictly later held-out test year, simple baselines with intervals, a household framing, and a Virginia focus. We do not claim temporal shifting as new. |
| Joseph, Savadi, Souza, "LiteCast: A Lightweight Forecaster for Carbon Optimizations", arXiv 2511.06187 (v2, Nov 2025) | Savings depend on ranking of hours more than forecast error; across 50 regions a lightweight forecaster reportedly gets 20% more savings than state-of-the-art forecasters and 97% of the attainable maximum. | Abstract opened | Our "better forecast is not a better choice" observation is consistent with this and is not new. Our additions: a held-out 2026 test, and the boundary condition that ranking-focused training helped only in the flattest grid (E1). |
| Radovanovic et al. (15 authors), "Carbon-Aware Computing for Datacenters", arXiv 2106.11750, submitted 11 Jun 2021 (Google), https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.11750 | Google's Carbon-Intelligent Compute Management: each day it gathers next-day carbon-intensity forecasts and day-ahead demand predictions and uses risk-aware optimization to set hourly Virtual Capacity Curves limiting resources for temporally flexible work, keeping total daily capacity so that work finishes within the day. Abstract gives no savings figures. | Abstract opened | Same decision (shift flexible work using a day-ahead forecast), but ours is an open public-data study with baselines, held-out testing and households. We do not claim the idea as new. |
| Sohrabbeig, Ardakanian, Musilek (University of Alberta), "Learning to Reduce Data Center Carbon Emissions via Forecast-Integrated Workload Scheduling", file hosted as `PESGM26.pdf`, https://sites.ualberta.ca/~oardakan/files/PESGM26.pdf (venue not stated in the text we read; the file name suggests IEEE PES General Meeting 2026, unverified) | Trains the carbon-intensity forecaster end to end for a differentiable emission-aware scheduler instead of predict-then-optimize; it places long AI training jobs across geo-distributed data centers (spatial, not temporal shifting). Using ElectricityMaps traces and three workload datasets it reports a 1-1.4% greater reduction in emissions-related relative regret than the best two-stage method, and that "higher forecast accuracy does not necessarily translate into lower emissions". | Full PDF text read | They shift jobs between sites and train end to end; we shift in time and test a simple decision-trained second stage across five grids, where it helped only in the flattest grid (E1). Their finding and ours agree that accuracy and decision quality can diverge. |
| Chen, Xu, Wu, Sun, "Prediction optimization fusion learning-based approach for day-ahead carbon aware scheduling in distribution network", Applied Energy 397, 2025, article 126369, https://doi.org/10.1016/j.apenergy.2025.126369 (listing: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/appene/v397y2025ics0306261925010992.html) | Predicts nodal carbon intensity with a sequence neural network and fuses it with a two-stage dispatch model for shiftable load; defines a "surrogate dynamic regret" (gap between the decision under predicted and under true intensity); claims better carbon-aware decisions than state-of-the-art methods at comparable prediction accuracy. No figures in the abstract. | Abstract only (full text not accessed) | Distribution-network operator setting with a neural dispatch model; ours is household and data-center start-time choice with gradient boosting. ## What is genuinely ours (say it modestly)
Public data only; five grids with one primary region (Northern Virginia); strictly later held-out test with four baselines and block-bootstrap intervals;
negative and mixed results kept (E1-E4); a household-facing tool. None of the underlying ideas (temporal shifting, ranking over error) is new.
